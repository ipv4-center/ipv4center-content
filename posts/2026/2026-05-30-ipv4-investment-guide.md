# Is IPv4 a Smart Investment in 2026? The Numbers Nobody Talks About

### A finite digital asset that pays you every month — here's the real math behind buying and leasing IPv4 addresses.

![Subtitle image suggestion: a glowing digital globe of network connections with rising charts. Caption: "IPv4 — the internet's scarce real estate."]

When people think about alternative investments, they reach for the usual list: stocks, real estate, gold, crypto. Almost nobody mentions the one asset that quietly powers the entire internet and has become genuinely scarce — **IPv4 addresses**.

Yet IPv4 ticks every box investors look for: it's **finite**, it's **transferable**, and — unlike a gold bar sitting in a vault — it **produces monthly income**. In this post I'll walk through the actual numbers, as of **30 May 2026**, so you can decide for yourself.

> ⚠️ This is educational content, not financial advice. Prices and returns vary with the market. Always do your own research.

---

## First: why is IPv4 even worth money?

The internet ran out of fresh IPv4 addresses years ago. There are only about **4.3 billion** of them, and no more are being created. Meanwhile demand never stopped: new cloud regions, ISPs, hosting companies, VPN providers, and enterprises all still need routable IPv4 to talk to the rest of the world.

Fixed supply + rising demand = a scarce asset. The closest analogy is **real estate**: you buy the "land," tenants pay you "rent" to use it, and you still own the asset the whole time.

The difference from physical property? IPv4 can be leased globally with almost no maintenance, no tenants trashing the walls, and no property tax.

---

## The entry point: a /24 subnet

The standard trading unit is a **/24** — that's **256 IP addresses**. Here's what it looks like today:

- **Cost:** ~$25 per IP → **~$6,400 total**
- **Lease income:** **$100–$150 per month**
- **Worst-case payback:** ~**64 months**

The math is refreshingly simple:

```
$6,400 ÷ $100/month = 64 months
```

After about 64 months, your block has fully paid for itself. But here's the part that makes it an *investment* rather than a one-time trade: **the income keeps coming, and you still own the asset.** At the higher end ($150/month), payback drops to roughly **43 months**.

---

## The compounding trick: annual contracts

In the real world, many tenants don't want to renegotiate every month. They want stability — so they sign **3-year or 5-year contracts** and pay **annually**.

Yes, annual prepayment usually means giving a **reasonable discount**. But in exchange you get a big chunk of cash *upfront* instead of small monthly trickles. And that upfront cash can immediately buy **another /24**.

That's how a single block snowballs into a portfolio:

```
Buy /24 #1 → lease (annual prepay) → cash → Buy /24 #2 → lease → cash → Buy /24 #3 ...
```

Each block helps finance the next. This is compounding, applied to the internet's address space.

## Going bigger: the /16

Here's where it gets really interesting. **Bulk blocks are cheaper per IP.**

A **/16** subnet is **65,536 IP addresses** — that's **256 separate /24 blocks** in one purchase. In today's market, the per-IP price drops to around **$15–$20**.

Let's run the numbers at $15/IP:

- **65,536 IPs × $15 ≈ $1,000,000** investment
- Leased out as 256 `/24` blocks at $100/month each → **256 × $100 = $25,600/month**
- **$983,040 ÷ $25,600 ≈ 39 months** to full payback

Read that again: a million-dollar IPv4 position can return its capital in **about 39 months** — and then keep paying. The bigger the block, the lower the per-IP cost, and the faster the amortization.

| Scenario | IPs | Price/IP | Total | Monthly income | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **/24** (entry) | 256 | $25 | ~$6,400 | $100–150 | ~64 months |
| **/16** (scale) | 65,536 | $15–20 | ~$1M | ~$25,600 | ~39 months |

## A word on risk

IPv4 is real, but it isn't risk-free. Before buying any block, check:

- **Reputation** — is the range on Spamhaus or major blocklists? Dirty IPs take months to clean up.
- **Ownership** — legacy/disputed blocks can have transfers reversed. Insist on proper registry (LIR) verification.
- **Routing security** — valid RPKI/ROA records protect against BGP hijacking.
- **Price sanity** — a block priced 30–40% below market is usually a warning, not a deal.

A price far below market is rarely cheap because the seller is generous. It's cheap because the address space carries baggage. Verify first.
